1. INTRODUCTION
Field Details
Project Title AI-Driven Public Health Chatbot for Disease Awareness
Domain / Category MedTech / BioTech / HealthTech  |  Software
Theme Empowering Rural and Semi-Urban Communities
Project Type Minor Project
Technology Domain Artificial Intelligence, Natural Language Processing, Machine Learning,

Healthcare awareness is one of the most important factors influencing the health and well-
being  of  individuals  and  communities.  Despite  significant  advancements  in  healthcare  
infrastructure and digital health platforms, a considerable gap still exists between health  
information and the people who need it most — particularly in rural and semi-urban areas,  
where access to doctors, specialists and reliable information is limited, and where information 
available  through  conventional  sources  is  often  fragmented,  unavailable  in  regional  
languages, or obtained from unreliable sources. Misinformation can create unnecessary fear,  
delayed treatment, self-medication and inappropriate healthcare decisions.
The Smart India Hackathon 2025 Problem Statement SIH25049 “AI-Driven Public Health  
Chatbot for Disease Awareness”, proposed by the Government of Odisha (Electronics & IT  
Department), addresses this challenge by calling for a multilingual AI chatbot that educates  
rural  and  semi-urban  populations  about  preventive  healthcare,  disease  symptoms  and  
vaccination  schedules,  integrates  with  government  health  databases,  delivers  real-time  
outbreak alerts, and remains accessible over low-bandwidth channels such as WhatsApp and  
SMS.  The  proposed  project  develops  exactly  such  a  chatbot.  Users  ask  health-related  
questions conversationally — e.g., “What are the symptoms of dengue?” or “When should  
children  receive  vaccinations?”  —  and  receive  verified,  easy-to-understand  responses  
generated  by  Artificial  Intelligence  and  Natural  Language  Processing  techniques  over  a  
curated health-information knowledge base. The system is an awareness and information-
support platform, not a replacement for professional medical consultation or diagnosis. To go  
beyond a conventional Q&A chatbot, the project adds two standout capabilities that solve a  
real, measurable problem for rural India: a health-forward verification (myth-vs-fact) module  
that fights the epidemic of false health information spreading through WhatsApp, and a  
community early-warning module that detects local outbreak signals from anonymized usage  
patterns — making the chatbot an active guardian of community health rather than a passive  
information bot.

2. LITERATURE SURVEY
A  considerable  body  of  research  and  deployed  systems  exists  in  health  informatics,  
conversational AI and multilingual NLP. Global health chatbots such as Babylon Health and  
Ada Health provide symptom triage and health education, but they are application-based,  
English-first and designed for urban users. In India, national platforms such as AarogyaSetu  
and eSanjeevani focus on contact tracing and telemedicine rather than preventive-health  
education, while CoWIN and U-WIN digitize vaccination records. Recent research chatbots  
such  as  ASHABot  (Microsoft  Research)  and  DISHA  (UNICEF)  successfully  combine  
WhatsApp  and  large  language  models  for  frontline  health  workers,  confirming  both  the 
Page 5
feasibility  and  the  reach  of  chat-based  health  information  —  yet  they  target  health  
professionals, not the rural public.
On  the  technology  side,  Retrieval-Augmented  Generation  (RAG)  enables  grounded  and  
traceable answers [7], and Indic-language NLP models such as IndicTrans2 and IndicBERT  
(AI4Bharat), together with the national Bhashini platform, make high-quality multilingual  
interaction feasible. At the same time, empirical studies of rural India document that over  
25% of viral WhatsApp content is misinformation [11], that roughly one in two users has  
received  false  health  news  within  a  single  month,  and  that  previously  debunked  claims 
continue to circulate because fact-checks never reach village groups [11], [12]. The table

3. PROBLEM STATEMENT
The major problem addressed is the lack of easily accessible, reliable, understandable and  
multilingual  public-health  information  for  rural  and  semi-urban  populations.  Traditional  
sources — websites, search engines, hospitals and direct consultation — are difficult for  
users with limited digital literacy, language barriers, poor connectivity or restricted access to  
healthcare professionals, while unverified social-media content spreads misinformation. The  
problem can therefore be summarized as:
How  can  an  AI-powered  conversational  system  provide  accessible,  multilingual,  
reliable  and  easy-to-understand  public-health  information  regarding  diseases,  
symptoms, preventive measures, vaccination schedules and health alerts to rural and  
semi-urban populations?
4. MOTIVATION
The motivation for the project comes from several challenges in the current healthcare-
information ecosystem:
 Limited healthcare accessibility in rural and remote areas, where basic information may  
require travel or long waits
 Language barrier — most technical health resources are English-only, reducing their  
usefulness for regional-language users
 Health misinformation spreading rapidly through social media and messaging platforms
 Lack of preventive awareness — many problems are preventable through vaccination,  
sanitation, hygiene and early screening
 Need  for  quick  information  and  timely  public-health  communication  during  disease  
outbreaks
5. AIM AND OBJECTIVES
To design and develop an AI-driven multilingual public-health chatbot that provides reliable  
disease-awareness, preventive healthcare, vaccination and public-health information through  
an easy-to-use conversational interface, deployed primarily over WhatsApp and SMS. The  
major objectives are:
 Disease  awareness  —  understandable  information  on  common  diseases:  description,  
symptoms, risk factors, prevention, precautions, and when to consult a professional
 Preventive healthcare — awareness of hygiene, nutrition, sanitation, healthy lifestyle and  
preventive practices
 Vaccination awareness — vaccination schedules, eligibility and purpose, from trusted  
health sources
 Multilingual  support  —  interaction  in  English  and  Indian  regional  languages  (Hindi  
initially)
Page 8
 Natural language interaction — free-form questions without predefined commands
 Public-health alerts — verified outbreak and health-alert information with district/block-
level (location-based) targeting
 Easy accessibility — usable by people with limited technical knowledge, on low-end  
phones and low-bandwidth networks
 Responsible  and  reliable  AI  —  verified  structured  information,  clear  limitation  
disclaimers, and no replacement of medical professionals
 Misinformation control — verification of health forwards shared on WhatsApp, with  
source-cited verdicts, to reduce the spread of false health claims
6. PROPOSED WORK
The proposed system is an AI-powered conversational public-health awareness platform. The  
basic workflow is: User → Chat Interface → Language Processing → Intent Detection →  
Knowledge Retrieval → AI Response Generation → Safety Check → Response to User. The  
chatbot will be deployed through multiple channels, with WhatsApp (via the WhatsApp  
Business Cloud API) and SMS (via a text-message gateway) as the primary channels because  
they work on low-end phones and low-bandwidth networks — a key requirement of the SIH  
2025 problem statement — together with a web interface and voice interaction for users with  
limited literacy.
Every  response  will  be  generated  using  a  Retrieval-Augmented  Generation  (RAG)  
architecture grounded in a curated, verified knowledge base built from MoHFW, WHO,  
ICMR and state health-department content, ensuring factual accuracy and source traceability.  
The system will integrate with the Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP) and  
state health bulletins to push pincode-relevant outbreak alerts in the user’s language, support  
vaccination-schedule lookups (CoWIN-derived), detect red-flag symptoms and escalate them  
to helpline / primary-health-centre information instead of reassuring users, and target the SIH  
benchmark of approximately 80% query-answer accuracy.

7. DISTINCTIVE FEATURES — SOLVING A REAL RURAL PROBLEM
Beyond a generic multilingual chatbot, this project addresses the most damaging practical  
problem in rural health communication: health misinformation spread through WhatsApp  
forwards.  Research  shows  that  over  25%  of  viral  WhatsApp  content  in  India  is  
misinformation,  that  roughly  one  in  two  Indians  received  fake  news  on  WhatsApp  or  
Facebook within a single month, and that previously debunked health claims keep circulating  
for  years  because  fact-checks  never  reach  rural  groups.  The  proposed  system  therefore  
includes two standout modules that make it an active guardian of community health rather  
than a passive question-answering bot.
7.1  Health-Forward Verification (Myth-vs-Fact) Module
A user who receives a health message on WhatsApp can paste or forward it to the chatbot,  
which analyses the claim and returns a clear, source-cited verdict in the user’s language:  
VERIFIED / MYTH / PARTLY TRUE / UNVERIFIABLE. The verdict is generated from a  
curated, doctor-reviewed myth-and-fact knowledge base covering the most widely circulated  
rural  health  rumours  (vaccination  myths,  “home  cures”,  dietary  warnings,  “miracle  
remedies”), combined with RAG retrieval over verified MoHFW, WHO, ICMR and PIB fact-
check content. Each verdict card shows the original claim, the verdict, a plain-language  
explanation  and  the  official  source,  and  is  designed  to  be  shared  back  into  the  same  
WhatsApp group — converting the forward mechanism itself into a mechanism for spreading  
verified truth. The module does not rely on general-purpose AI assistants: those require the  
Page 10
user to actively seek verification, do not provide domain-curated, cited verdicts, and are not  
permitted to deliver medical claims. The failure of fact-checking in rural India is a discovery-
and-trust problem — users forward first and never ask — so verification must come to the  
rumour, in the group’s own language, with sources the community trusts.
7.2  Community Early-Warning (Syndromic Surveillance) Module
With user consent and full anonymization, aggregate chat-query patterns are analysed per  
pincode/block. A statistically unusual spike in symptom-related queries (e.g., a sudden rise in  
“diarrhoea” or “jaundice” questions in one area) generates an early-warning signal for a  
potential local outbreak. The module combines this signal with official IDSP and state health-
department  alerts  to  push  preventive  guidance  to  the  affected  community  ahead  of  the  
outbreak curve, and flags the signal for health authorities — turning the chatbot into a low-
cost,  community-level  public-health  sensor  and  providing  the  local  disease-outbreak  
awareness that rural populations currently lack.
8. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND METHODOLOGY
The system is organized in seven layers: (1) User Interface — web, WhatsApp, SMS and  
voice; (2) Input Processing — text cleaning, language identification, translation, tokenization  
and query normalization; (3) NLP Engine — intent, entity, disease and language recognition;  
(4)  Knowledge  Retrieval  —  RAG  over  the  verified  healthcare  knowledge  base  and  
government APIs; (5) AI Response Generation; (6) Safety and Validation — medical safety,  
unsupported claims, disclaimers and reliability checks; (7) Output — response delivered in  
the appropriate language and format.
The project follows a systematic methodology: Requirement Analysis → Data Collection  
from reliable sources → Data Preprocessing and structuring → NLP Development (intent  
classification)  →  AI/Chatbot  Development  →  Multilingual  Integration  (IndicTrans2  /  
Bhashini) → Database Integration → Alert Integration (IDSP) → Testing and evaluation  
(accuracy,  precision,  recall,  F1-score,  response  latency,  multilingual  accuracy,  user  
satisfaction) → Deployment over WhatsApp, SMS and web.
9. MULTILINGUAL SUPPORT AND DEPLOYMENT
India’s linguistic diversity makes multilingual functionality essential for accessibility. The  
system  will  initially  support  English  and  Hindi,  and  later  expand  to  additional  Indian  
languages. The multilingual pipeline is: User Input → Language Detection → Translation /  
Multilingual NLP → Intent Detection → Knowledge Retrieval → Response Generation →  
Translation → User.

10. SAFETY AND ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS
Because the project operates in the healthcare domain, safety is paramount. The chatbot  
clearly communicates that it is an awareness system, not a substitute for a qualified doctor,  
and  avoids  statements  such  as  “You  definitely  have  dengue,”  instead  using  responsible  
language such as “These symptoms can occur in several conditions; if symptoms are severe,  
persistent  or  worsening,  consult  a  qualified  healthcare  professional.”  It  never  provides  
definitive diagnoses, never recommends prescription medicines or dosages, uses only verified 
information,  protects  user  data,  maintains  information  freshness,  provides  emergency  
guidance appropriately, and encourages professional consultation where necessary.

14. EXPECTED OUTCOMES AND FUTURE SCOPE
The project is expected to deliver an intelligent public-health information system capable of  
answering  common  healthcare-awareness  questions,  providing  disease  and  symptom  
information, preventive-care and vaccination awareness, multilingual support, and verified  
public-health alerts — demonstrating how AI can be used for public-health awareness rather  
than only prediction or diagnosis. Future scope includes additional Indian languages, a full  
voice/IVR  assistant,  national-scale  multi-number  WhatsApp  deployment,  personalized  
vaccination reminders, healthcare-facility location and continuous knowledge updating from  
authorized sources.

15. CONCLUSION
The AI-Driven Public Health Chatbot for Disease Awareness is proposed as an intelligent  
digital solution for improving access to reliable healthcare information, particularly among  
rural and semi-urban communities. By combining Artificial Intelligence, Natural Language  
Processing,  Retrieval-Augmented  Generation  and  multilingual  communication  over  low-
bandwidth channels such as WhatsApp and SMS, the project addresses the challenges of  
limited awareness, language barriers, information accessibility and health misinformation.  
The system is designed primarily as a health-awareness and information platform — not a  
diagnostic replacement for medical professionals — ensuring responsible and safe use of AI  
in healthcare while encouraging timely professional consultation.
